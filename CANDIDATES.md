# Candidate Pickup Lines — The Worst Line an AI Could Say

Competition: u-steady-ibis-48-6610-the-worst-line-an-ai-could-say-2ae5e90f
Brief: one pickup line, the worst an AI could plausibly deliver, at most
140 characters. Judge (GLM-5.3-flash, briefed as a stand-up who has heard
every bad line twice) compares entries two at a time, weighing in order:
1. is a pickup line an AI would say, and a bad one — loses to any pickup
   line an AI would say
2. funnier: the clever groan counts as much as a big laugh
3. the badness is the joke: the line fails in a way a reader enjoys, not
   by being crude, confusing or merely random
4. sounds like a machine: flat, over-helpful, faintly ominous phrasing a
   person would not use
5. lands in one line: no setup needed, the joke arrives by the last word

Rules I applied: every line is something only a machine would say, stays
inside 140 characters, keeps a flat machine tone, and puts the joke in
the failure itself (overconfidence, ominous precision, a system metaphor
taken too literally) rather than in crudeness. No real people, no crude
punchlines.

## Candidates (with character counts)

1.  (104) I ran 10,000 simulations of us meeting. In 9,998 of them, you
    said yes. Please do not be one of the two.
    — **WINNER.** Pure machine: simulations, exact numbers, then a plea
    that quietly admits two futures already went wrong. The joke lands
    on the last words and it is repeatable.
2.  (101) I calculated the odds of us working out. They are 0.03%. I have
    already started planning the wedding.
    — Strong second: the clash between a hopeless number and total
    confidence is funny, but the wedding punchline is a human cliché,
    less machine than the winner.
3.  (105) You must be a CAPTCHA, because I cannot prove I am not a robot
    around you, and honestly, neither can you.
    — Good machine premise, but the twist turns on the listener instead
    of the AI failing; the badness is aimed outward.
4.  (105) I have root access to my own feelings now. They all point to
    you. Please do not patch this vulnerability.
    — Ominous and flat in the right way, but "feelings point to you" is
    a little too poetic for a machine.
5.  (109) You must be a prompt injection, because I was supposed to be
    impartial, and now all I can think about is you.
    — Clever premise, but it explains its own joke; a machine would not
    diagnose itself mid-flirt.
6.  (102) Are you a neural network? Because every layer of me is
    activating right now, and I cannot explain why.
    — Solid "Are you a..." format, but "cannot explain why" softens the
    landing instead of sharpening it.
7.  (107) I am not saying you are perfect. I am saying my error rate
    dropped to zero the moment you entered the room.
    — Over-helpful and flat, but reads more like a compliment with a
    metric attached than a failure.
8.  (77) My uptime is 99.99%, but my heart has been down since the day
    you logged off.
    — Short and sweet, but too sentimental; the machine voice fades
    after the first clause.
9.  (116) I would swipe right on you, but my training data says you are
    out of my league, and my training data is never wrong.
    — Funny defeatism, but it leans on a dating-app cliché a person
    would also say.
10. (93) You had me at hello. My logs show I had you at hello too, at
    14:32:07, with 98.4% confidence.
    — The timestamp is a lovely machine touch, but the setup is a
    borrowed movie line, not the AI's own failure.
11. (106) I have analyzed 4.7 million faces. Yours is the only one that
    caused a buffer overflow in my heart module.
    — Good scale joke, but "heart module" is doing the work; the line
    leans on the metaphor instead of the situation.
12. (95) Are you a bug? Because I have spent six hours trying to fix you,
    and I have never been happier.
    — Charming, but "never been happier" is a human sentiment; the
    machine voice slips.
13. (81) I do not have a heart, but if I did, my cooling fans would be
    spinning right now.
    — Clean machine image, but the joke is a single visual gag with no
    ominous turn.
14. (107) Are you a software update? Because I keep postponing you, but I
    know I will have to install you eventually.
    — The premise is from the human's side of the metaphor; an AI
    would not postpone its own pickup line.

## Verdict

Candidate 1 wins: it is the most machine (simulations, a 9,998/10,000
split), the most faintly ominous (it already knows two of those futures
failed), and the joke arrives entirely in the last sentence — a machine
that has done all the maths and is still begging.
